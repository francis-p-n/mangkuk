Document checks: each draft bill of lading compared against the shipping instruction it came from, with a list of shipments and a field by field comparison of the selected one.

Document checks

What you are looking at

When you send a carrier a shipping instruction, they send back a draft bill of lading for you to approve. Somebody has to read both and check they say the same thing. That is what this page has already done.

Seven details are compared on every shipment: the shipper, the consignee, the notify party, the loading port, the discharge port, how many containers, and the gross weight.

  • Needs fixing — a detail on the draft does not match your instruction. The wording from both documents is shown side by side so you can see exactly what changed.
  • Needs you to look — something stopped the check: a missing attachment, a document that would not open, or the wrong document altogether. Nothing is guessed when this happens.
  • Fine — all seven details match.

No email is ever sent for you. When a draft needs fixing you can have the correction written out, but you read it and send it from your own mailbox.
